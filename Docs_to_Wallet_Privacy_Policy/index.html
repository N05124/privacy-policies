Docs2Wallet

Privacy Policy · Updated October 14, 2026

This policy describes how the Docs2Wallet iOS app handles information when you turn a document you already have into a personal Apple Wallet pass. Docs2Wallet is published by Arison. The pass is signed with an Apple Pass Type ID certificate issued to Apple Developer Team ID MDV83ZYNR6.

Docs2Wallet makes a personal copy for your own Wallet. The pass states on its back that it was created from your document and was not issued by the event organizer.

Information that stays on your device

You can import a screenshot, a PDF, pasted text, or a ticket link you already have. Docs2Wallet reads that material on your iPhone to suggest pass fields. That reading uses Apple frameworks on the device, including Vision for text and barcodes. When your iPhone supports Apple Intelligence, Docs2Wallet also uses it through the on-device Foundation Models framework. The screenshot, PDF, and extracted text are not uploaded for that reading.

When you import a ticket link, Docs2Wallet downloads that page from its website, the way a browser would, and reads it on your iPhone. That website sees an ordinary request for the page. If you choose to try loading the page in a web view, it is loaded without keeping cookies or a cache.

You can also send material to Docs2Wallet from another app with the share sheet: up to three images, one file or PDF, text, or a link, plus an optional note. The share extension places a temporary copy in the app’s own storage on your iPhone, and Docs2Wallet deletes that copy when it imports it the next time you open the app.

Passes you confirm are listed in My Passes only until you quit the app. Docs2Wallet does not save them. The copy you keep is the one in Wallet.

Calendar is optional. After a pass is handed to Wallet, you can save a matching event with its title, location, notes and link. Docs2Wallet asks only for permission to add events, not to read your calendar. You can instead share the event as an .ics file, which is prepared in the app’s temporary folder on your iPhone. Calendar contents are not sent to the signing service.

Information used to sign a pass

You review the pass before it is signed. Only after you confirm does Docs2Wallet send the fields for that pass to the signing service. The original screenshot, PDF, photos, or full extraction text are not part of that request.

The signing service builds the .pkpass package, signs it with the Pass Type ID certificate, and returns the file to your device. It does not keep a database of your documents, passes, or Apple ID. The request exists for that signing step and is not retained as a stored pass.

To make sure signing requests come from a genuine copy of Docs2Wallet, the app uses Apple’s App Attest service. The first time you sign a pass, your iPhone creates an App Attest key with Apple and sends the signing service the key’s identifier and Apple’s attestation. Each later request carries a one-time proof made with that key. The key identifier is not your Apple ID and does not identify you. The signing service keeps it so it can recognise this installation of the app, and your iPhone keeps it in the app’s Keychain.

The signing certificate

Apple Wallet accepts a pass when the pass is signed by a Pass Type ID certificate that Apple issued to a developer account. Docs2Wallet uses one certificate for the passes it creates. You can see the same identifiers on the certificate in Apple Developer, under Certificates, Identifiers & Profiles, where it is listed as a Pass Type ID Certificate.

Those two identifiers are written into every pass.json the app signs. passTypeIdentifier must match the Pass Type ID on the certificate. teamIdentifier must match the Team ID on the certificate. Wallet uses that match to attribute the pass to this developer account. The identifiers are not your Apple ID, and they are not the name of the venue printed on the pass. The venue name comes from the document you confirmed. The certificate name is the developer account that signed the file.

The private key for the certificate stays with signing. It is not placed in the pass, and it is not sent to you.

Signing covers the files in the pass package. Docs2Wallet writes manifest.json with a SHA-1 hash of each file, then creates a detached PKCS #7 signature over that manifest with the Pass Type ID certificate. The signature uses SHA-256 and includes a signing time. The public certificate is embedded in the signature so Wallet can check it through Apple’s Worldwide Developer Relations intermediate certificate. When the check succeeds, Wallet treats the text and images in the package as issued under this Pass Type ID.

The certificate binds the pass you approved to Team ID MDV83ZYNR6 and Pass Type ID pass.com.threadedrealm.doc-wallet. It does not insert a separate copy of your personal information. Personal details appear in the pass only when they are part of the fields or images you confirmed.

What the signed pass contains

The signature covers pass.json and the images in the package. Depending on the document you confirmed, pass.json can include:

  • Format version 1, the Pass Type ID, the Team ID, and a serial number. The serial number is a unique identifier generated for that pass.
  • A description and an organization name taken from the document, such as the event or venue name.
  • The pass style you are creating, such as an event ticket, boarding pass, store card, coupon, or generic pass.
  • Fields you confirmed: event title, organization, venue, address, date and time, doors, additional event days, seating (section, row, seat, or gate), a short details summary, and reference values read from the document.
  • Ticket, event, or organizer URLs, including a tappable link so you can open the original page if a code will not scan.
  • A barcode or QR payload and its label, when the code uses a format Wallet can draw. Other code payloads from the same document may be placed as text on the back of the pass.
  • A relevant date, so Wallet can surface the pass around the event time.
  • Places: the venue from the document, or up to ten venues you choose with place search, each with a latitude, a longitude, and a short label, and text Wallet can show on the Lock Screen near them. Docs2Wallet does not collect your iPhone’s location for this.
  • Colors used to draw the pass.
  • A fixed note on the back saying the pass was created from your own document and was not issued by the event organizer.

The package also includes the pass icon, which the signing service adds. Those image files are hashed and covered by the same signature. Photos and screenshots you import are not placed in the pass.

A document can contain personal details. Before a pass is signed, Docs2Wallet runs a privacy filter on your iPhone that leaves out details such as ID and passport numbers, payment card and bank numbers, personal addresses, phone numbers, email addresses and dates of birth. Attendee names are left off unless you turn them on for that pass. For some details the app warns you and you can choose Include anyway. The filter works by recognising patterns, so it can miss something: check the fields on the review screen. Whatever you leave in the fields you confirm is inside the signed pass and is covered by the certificate in the way described above.

What the certificate does not authorize

The Pass Type ID certificate lets Wallet trust this pass as signed by this developer team. It does not give Docs2Wallet access to the other passes in your Wallet, your Apple ID, iCloud, or apps other than the photos, files, and calendar events you choose inside Docs2Wallet.

The signed pass does not include a webServiceURL or an authenticationToken. Your device does not call Docs2Wallet later to refresh the pass, and Docs2Wallet does not receive a device library identifier or a push token for it. The pass also does not include an NFC payload.

The certificate record in the Apple Developer portal lists the Pass Type ID and the team. It does not list your tickets.

Docs2Wallet does not use the information from your document to track you across apps or websites, and it does not use it for advertising. Docs2Wallet does not sell that information.

Place search

When you search for a venue to add to a pass, the text you type is sent to Apple Maps to find matching places. Docs2Wallet does not add your location to that search and does not ask for location permission. Apple’s handling of Maps searches is described in Apple’s Privacy Policy.

Purchases

Your first passes each year are free. A one-time purchase through the App Store unlocks more. Apple handles the payment, and Docs2Wallet does not receive your payment details. The app checks with the App Store on your iPhone whether your account owns the unlock.

To count free passes, Docs2Wallet keeps a small counter in the Keychain on this iPhone. It is not synced or sent anywhere, and it may remain on the iPhone after the app is deleted.

Retention and sharing

On the signing service, the confirmed pass is processed to produce the file that returns to your iPhone and is not kept as a stored pass. The signing service keeps the App Attest key identifier described above. On your iPhone, passes are listed in the app only until you quit it; the pass stays in Wallet until you remove it there.

Apple receives the pass when you add it to Wallet. Apple’s handling of Wallet is described in Apple’s Privacy Policy. Docs2Wallet does not send Apple the original screenshot as part of signing.

Your choices

  • Edit or remove fields on the review screen before you confirm the pass, and decide whether to include attendee names.
  • Decline calendar access, or share an .ics file instead.
  • Skip place search and keep only the venue from your document, or none.
  • Remove the pass from Wallet at any time.

Docs2Wallet is not directed to children under 13. Where privacy law gives you a right to ask about the information described here, email the address below. Because the signing service does not keep the pass, deletion of a pass you already created is done in Wallet. Deleting the app removes what it stores on your iPhone, except the free-pass counter described above.
